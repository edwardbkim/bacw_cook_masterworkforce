Key Findings
Housing · Critical

Homeownership is structurally unaffordable in two of three markets

Bloomington (−$8.31/hr) and Winston-Salem (−$7.98/hr) production workers cannot afford to buy a home on a single income at the local production median wage. This isn't a wage problem; it's a structural retention risk. Workers who can't build equity are workers who leave. Canton is the only Cook market where the math works: +$0.80/hr over the homeownership threshold, on a $107,100 median home value.

Bloomington · Opportunity

Novo Nordisk's exit creates a rare talent injection

~400 skilled biomanufacturing workers will enter the Bloomington labor market in May 2026. The Assembler LQ of 3.97 means Bloomington already has the deepest concentration of this exact worker profile in the country. Cook should move immediately: Simtra BioPharma, which is expanding in the same market, will be absorbing from the same pool.

Winston-Salem · Scale

Winston-Salem is Cook's most scalable long-term recruiting market

22,950 production workers, +7.4% population growth, 11.3% mobility rate (vs. 21.8% in Bloomington). The challenge here isn't access, it's differentiation. Reynolds American (2,500 employees) sets the effective wage floor in Forsyth County. Cook must benchmark against RAI, not just OEWS medians, and compete on career pathway, stability, and brand.

Canton · Retention

Canton's affordability edge should anchor the retention strategy

Canton workers can afford a home at median production wages, something no Cook worker in Bloomington or Winston-Salem can do on a single income. This is a concrete, communicable retention differentiator. The risk is structural: the population has declined 11.5% since 2015. Cook's Canton strategy must prioritize tenure over volume. Caterpillar's UAW wage history shapes expectations; Cook competes on stability and medical device prestige.

Bloomington, IN · Monroe + Owen Counties
Life Sciences Cluster: Concentrated, Competitive, and Active
Bloomington is the #1 small MSA for medical device employment concentration and #2 for biopharma. Cook competes inside a deep but narrow specialist pool.
Novo Nordisk (fka Catalent)
Biopharmaceutical Manufacturing
⚡ Active Event
~1,800 → ~1,400 workers ~400 layoffs effective May 2026
What This Means for Cook

Recruitment window: act now. These are not generic production workers. They are trained biomanufacturing professionals: sterile fill/finish operators, QA analysts, validation specialists, and maintenance mechanics. Their skills map directly onto medical device manufacturing. Cook should stand up a targeted outreach campaign before Simtra, Boston Scientific, and other area employers absorb this pool.

Retention Signal

Novo's regulatory troubles (FDA warning letter, Jul 2025) and global restructuring signal pharma industry volatility. Cook's medical device stability is a credible counternarrative for workers who want out of the pharma cycle.

Sources Xtalks / BioSpace: Novo Bloomington layoffs (Apr 2026) · Indiana DWD WARN Act filings · Novo Nordisk newsroom · FDA warning letters
Simtra BioPharma Solutions
Biopharmaceutical Manufacturing
High Threat: Expanding
~500 → ~630 workers $241M expansion · +130 jobs (Aug 2025)

Simtra is Cook's most acute ongoing competitor in Bloomington. They are growing not contracting. Their production specialist and QA analyst postings run $22–38/hr, above Cook's likely range. 33 active roles on Indeed confirm persistent hiring activity from the same labor pool Cook draws from.

What This Means for Cook

Simtra's expansion directly compresses the Bloomington production labor supply at exactly the moment Novo's layoffs temporarily expand it. Cook needs to move quickly on Novo's displaced workers, Simtra will be recruiting the same people.

Sources Indiana Daily Student, Simtra expansion (Aug 2025) · Simtra newsroom · Indiana Economic Development Corp.

Tobacco Manufacturing
High: Sets Wage Floor
2,500 Forsyth Co. employees Fortune 500 · Full BAT benefits

Reynolds American is the single largest manufacturing employer in Forsyth County. As a BAT subsidiary, it offers compensation and benefits that set the practical wage ceiling for blue-collar production workers in the Winston-Salem MSA.

What This Means for Cook

Critical benchmark: Cook's wage strategy in Winston-Salem must be benchmarked against Reynolds American, not just OEWS medians. The $20.08/hr production median understates what experienced production workers expect.

Caterpillar Country: Wage Expectations Set by UAW, Pool Defined by CAT Cycles
Caterpillar's East Peoria complex defines what blue-collar manufacturing wages look like in this MSA. Cook competes against a UAW-calibrated wage expectation that exceeds its likely pay range.
Caterpillar Inc.
Heavy Equipment Manufacturing
High: Wage Benchmark Threat
~10,000 Peoria area workers $25–45/hr · UAW · 123 active postings

Caterpillar's East Peoria and Mossville operations define the Peoria labor market. UAW-represented production workers earn $25–45/hr with industry-leading benefits.

Recruitment Opportunity (Cyclical)

CAT layoff cycles periodically release highly skilled machinists, operators, and maintenance workers. Cook should focus on workers who want off the CAT cycle permanently, not those waiting for a recall.

Retention Risk

Any Cook Canton worker with prior CAT experience is a CAT recall risk. Medical device mission, stability narrative, and non-cyclical employment are the key differentiators.

Sources Caterpillar newsroom · Caterpillar 10-K (SEC EDGAR) · UAW Local 974, CAT bargaining unit · Fulton County IL workforce statistics

Recommendations for the CFO

Six moves, ranked by time-to-act.

Numbered in order of urgency. The Bloomington recommendations compress into weeks; Canton retention is multi-year. Each is framed to answer the question: what would a CFO need to greenlight this?

01
Bloomington · Immediate

Activate a Novo Nordisk recruitment campaign, now

~400 skilled biomanufacturing workers enter the market in May 2026. Simtra BioPharma is expanding and will recruit from the same pool. Cook has a narrow window. Stand up targeted outreach: LinkedIn InMail campaigns, local job fair presence, direct referral incentives. Prioritize QA analysts, production technicians, and maintenance mechanics.

Time to act: weeks, not months →
02
Bloomington · Retention

Address the −$8.31/hr mortgage gap with targeted housing assistance

Bloomington city workers cannot buy a home at the median production wage. Ellettsville and Spencer, where Cook also operates, have materially better affordability (+$4.98/hr and +$6.98/hr rent gaps respectively). Consider housing assistance programs, down payment support, or employer-assisted mortgage initiatives.

Frame as a retention ROI, not a benefit cost →
03
Winston-Salem · Wages

Benchmark against Reynolds American not OEWS medians

Reynolds American (2,500 Forsyth Co. employees, BAT benefits) sets the effective wage floor. Cook's offers priced at OEWS median ($19.08/hr assembler) will consistently lose candidates. The assembler posted range is already $22–24/hr, $3–5 above OEWS. Price offers at or above the posted market.

Compensate at posted range, not OEWS benchmark →
04
Winston-Salem · Opportunity

Monitor Hanesbrands/Gildan for displacement events

Gildan's December 2025 acquisition of Hanesbrands (2,400 WS employees) creates workforce uncertainty. Post-acquisition restructuring is common. Hanesbrands assembly and packaging workers have directly transferable dexterous manufacturing skills. Establish a monitoring cadence, any Gildan restructuring announcement is a recruitment trigger.

Set up job alert monitoring for Hanesbrands layoff news →
05
Canton · Retention

Lead with the homeownership story — it's Cook's most unique retention asset

Canton is the only Cook US location where production wages clear the homeownership threshold. No competitor in any of Cook's markets can make this claim at comparable wages. This should be the centerpiece of Canton retention materials, recruitment ads, and manager talking points.

Deploy in job postings, offer letters, and onboarding →
06
Canton · Long-Horizon

Build for retention depth, not hiring volume — the pool is contracting

Canton's population has declined 11.5% since 2015. The labor pool is not going to replenish. Any Cook worker who leaves Canton is harder to replace than five years ago. Prioritize CAT workers who have permanently left the CAT cycle, not those between recall cycles.

Identify "permanently off the CAT cycle" in screening →
